How Many Calories Do You Really Need to Lose Weight?

Calorie targets can feel like guesswork. Here is a simple way to work out a sensible number for your body, without extreme cuts.

JG Janani Gokul Sandar 11 Oct 2026 6 min read

"How many calories should I eat?" is the question I hear most often from new clients. The honest answer is that it depends on your body, your daily routine and your goal. The good news is that you can get a solid starting estimate in a few minutes, and then fine-tune it as you go.

Step 1: Know your BMR

Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body uses just to keep you alive: breathing, circulating blood, repairing cells and keeping you warm. Even if you stayed in bed all day, you would burn roughly this many calories.

BMR depends mainly on your weight, height, age and sex. A commonly used formula is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is the one my calorie calculator uses.

Step 2: Add your activity to find your TDEE

Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. This is the number of calories that keeps your weight roughly steady, also called your maintenance calories.

Activity levelWhat it looks likeBMR multiplier
SedentaryLittle or no exercise, desk job× 1.2
Lightly activeLight exercise 1–3 days a week× 1.375
Moderately activeModerate exercise 3–5 days a week× 1.55
Very activeHard exercise 6–7 days a week× 1.725
Extra activePhysical job or twice-daily training× 1.9
Be honest about your activity level Most people overestimate how active they are. If you are unsure, pick the lower option. You can always adjust upwards later.

Step 3: Create a modest calorie deficit

To lose weight, you eat a little less than your TDEE. A common starting point is a deficit of about 500 calories a day, which works out to roughly 0.45 kg (1 lb) of weight loss per week. This is an approximation, not a promise. Bodies vary, and weight also moves with water, salt and hormones from day to day.

A worked example

A 30-year-old woman, 165 cm tall, weighing 70 kg, who exercises 3–5 days a week:

  • BMR: about 1,420 kcal per day
  • TDEE (× 1.55 for moderate activity): about 2,200 kcal per day
  • Starting target for weight loss (TDEE − 500): about 1,700 kcal per day

Why a smaller deficit is usually smarter

It is tempting to slash your intake to speed things up. In practice, a very large deficit tends to leave you hungry, tired and low on energy, and that makes it much harder to stay consistent. A moderate deficit that you can follow for months beats an aggressive one that you abandon after two weeks.

  • Start small. A deficit of 250–500 calories a day is enough for most people.
  • Eat enough protein and fibre. They keep you fuller for longer.
  • Move more where you can. Walking adds up and costs you very little effort.

Step 4: Check, then adjust

Treat your number as a starting point. Follow it for two to three weeks and watch the trend, not a single day's weigh-in. If your weight is not moving at all, trim your intake slightly or add a bit more activity. If you feel exhausted or constantly hungry, you may be eating too little, so ease the deficit.
